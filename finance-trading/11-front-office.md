
## Introduction

The **Front Office** is the part of a financial institution that is closest to the actual **investment, trading, and revenue-generating activities**.

In simple words:

> **Front Office = The people and systems that make investment and trading decisions and execute trades.**

A simplified financial organization can be viewed as:

```text
                FINANCIAL INSTITUTION
                        |
        +---------------+---------------+
        |               |               |
        ↓               ↓               ↓
   FRONT OFFICE    MIDDLE OFFICE    BACK OFFICE
        |               |               |
     Trading          Risk          Settlement
     Sales            Control       Accounting
     Research         Compliance    Reconciliation
```

The exact responsibilities vary between banks, brokerages, asset managers, hedge funds, and other financial institutions.

---

# 1. What Does Front Office Mean?

Front Office generally refers to business functions that:

- Interact directly with markets
- Make investment decisions
- Manage portfolios
- Execute trades
- Interact with clients
- Generate revenue
- Manage trading strategies

Examples include:

- Trading
- Sales
- Investment Banking
- Research
- Portfolio Management
- Asset Management

Not every institution has all of these functions.

---

# 2. Why Is It Called "Front Office"?

Think about a traditional company.

The people directly interacting with customers are considered the **front** of the business.

Similarly, in financial markets, the Front Office is close to:

```text
Clients
   ↓
Markets
   ↓
Trading
   ↓
Revenue
```

Middle and Back Office functions support and control these activities.

---

# 3. Front Office vs Middle Office vs Back Office

This distinction is extremely important.

| Area | Main Purpose |
|--------|-------------|
| Front Office | Make decisions and execute business |
| Middle Office | Control, monitor, and manage risk |
| Back Office | Process, settle, reconcile, and record |

### Simple Memory Trick

```text
FRONT  = TRADE
MIDDLE = CONTROL
BACK   = PROCESS
```

# 52. Execution Algorithms

Large orders may be divided into smaller orders using execution algorithms.

Common algorithmic concepts include:

- TWAP
- VWAP
- Implementation Shortfall
- Participation / POV

These strategies have different objectives and assumptions.

---

# 53. TWAP

**TWAP = Time Weighted Average Price**

A large order can be distributed across time.

Example:

```text
Total Order = 10,000

10:00 → 2,000
10:10 → 2,000
10:20 → 2,000
10:30 → 2,000
10:40 → 2,000
```

The goal is generally to spread execution over a specified time period.

---

# 54. VWAP

**VWAP = Volume Weighted Average Price**

Execution is generally shaped around expected market volume.

Conceptually:

```text
Market Volume
      ↓
Volume Profile
      ↓
Execution Schedule
      ↓
Orders
```

The objective is commonly to achieve execution near the market's volume-weighted average price over the relevant period.

---

# 55. Implementation Shortfall

Implementation Shortfall compares actual execution performance with a benchmark related to the decision or arrival price.

Conceptually:

```text
Decision Price
      ↓
Expected Transaction
      ↓
Actual Execution
      ↓
Cost / Shortfall
```

It helps evaluate the cost of executing a trading decision.

# 52. Execution Algorithms

Large orders may be divided into smaller orders using execution algorithms.

Common algorithmic concepts include:

- TWAP
- VWAP
- Implementation Shortfall
- Participation / POV

These strategies have different objectives and assumptions.

---

# 53. TWAP

**TWAP = Time Weighted Average Price**

A large order can be distributed across time.

Example:

```text
Total Order = 10,000

10:00 → 2,000
10:10 → 2,000
10:20 → 2,000
10:30 → 2,000
10:40 → 2,000
```

The goal is generally to spread execution over a specified time period.

---

# 54. VWAP

**VWAP = Volume Weighted Average Price**

Execution is generally shaped around expected market volume.

Conceptually:

```text
Market Volume
      ↓
Volume Profile
      ↓
Execution Schedule
      ↓
Orders
```

The objective is commonly to achieve execution near the market's volume-weighted average price over the relevant period.

---

# 55. Implementation Shortfall

Implementation Shortfall compares actual execution performance with a benchmark related to the decision or arrival price.

Conceptually:

```text
Decision Price
      ↓
Expected Transaction
      ↓
Actual Execution
      ↓
Cost / Shortfall
```

It helps evaluate the cost of executing a trading decision.
